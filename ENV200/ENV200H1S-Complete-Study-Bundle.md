## 🧠 The Core Learning Model (How to Actually Master This)

### Level 1: Definitions (Surface)
"Thermodynamics is... [memorized definition]"
❌ This is not enough for university exams.

### Level 2: Mechanisms (Deep)
"The 2nd Law says energy degrades to heat. In a trophic pyramid, this means 90% is lost at each step, so there can't be many predators."
✅ This is exam-level thinking.

### Level 3: Application (Expert)
"Given a new ecosystem scenario, I can explain population ratios using 2nd Law, and distinguish it from biological/genetic explanations."
✅ This is top-percentile performance.

**Your goal**: Reach **Level 2 (Mechanisms)** for all 10 core concepts by exam day.

**How to get there**:
1. Read the concept explanation in Master Study Plan (5 min)
2. Study the "mental model" or diagram (5 min)
3. Do a practice question on that topic (10 min)
4. Explain it out loud (3 min) — this forces you to articulate it

## ⏰ Time Management During Exam (90 minutes)

**Timing breakdown:**

| Section | Time | Questions |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Skim all Qs | 5 min | - |
| Multiple Choice | 20 min | 10 Q × 2 min avg |
| Short Answers | 35 min | 2 Q × 17.5 min |
| Review/Final checks | 30 min | All |

**Strategy:**
1. **First 5 min**: Skim all questions. Mark the 2-3 MC you're unsure about.
2. **Next 20 min**: Answer all MC in order. Skip the hard ones; circle them.
3. **Next 35 min**: Answer short answers with structure (Intro → Body → Conclusion).
4. **Last 30 min**:
   - Go back to hard MC; use elimination
   - Proofread short answers
   - Check formulas (IPAT, footprint)

**Key rule**: Don't spend >3 min on any one MC. Move on and come back.

## 🎓 How to Know You're Ready

### Signs You're Ready (Go into exam with confidence):
- ✅ You can explain 2nd Law using trophic pyramids **without notes**
- ✅ You can draw a Hadley Cell and label the deserts
- ✅ You know demographic transition lag (death falls first, birth lags)
- ✅ You **immediately** recognize "ozone hole = climate change" as FALSE
- ✅ You can contrast inductive vs. deductive reasoning with examples
- ✅ You know why seasons are about tilt, not distance (and can prove it with Jan/July)
- ✅ You can calculate IPAT mentally (population × consumption × technology)
- ✅ You can distinguish positive (amplifying) from negative (stabilizing) feedback
- ✅ You practice exam: >80% accuracy

### Signs You Need More Study:
- ❌ You're still memorizing definitions without understanding mechanisms
- ❌ You confuse ozone depletion with climate change
- ❌ You think "positive feedback = good"
- ❌ You're not sure why predators are rare
- ❌ Practice exam: <75% accuracy
- ❌ You can't articulate concepts in your own words

**If you see red flags**: Do targeted review on that specific topic (15-30 min) + another practice question.
