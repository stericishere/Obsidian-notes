# ENV200H1S Exam Trap Detector 🚨
**Use this reference DURING the exam if a question feels confusing. These are the 7 most likely traps.**

---

## TRAP #1: "Positive Feedback = Good" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "Which of the following is a positive feedback loop in the climate system?"
- Student thinks: "Positive = Good, so I should pick the beneficial option"
- **WRONG!** In systems science, "positive" means **amplifying**, which often leads to bad outcomes.

**How to Spot It:**
- Look for words: "positive feedback," "amplify," "accelerate," "runaway"
- **If it causes runaway change → it's positive feedback**

**Correct Thinking:**

| Type | Meaning | Effect |
|------|---------|--------|
| Positive | Amplifies change | Destabilizing |
| Negative | Dampens change | Stabilizing |

**Example:**
- Ice melting → lower albedo → more heat → more melting = **POSITIVE** (destabilizing, BAD)
- Predator-prey cycles = **NEGATIVE** (oscillates, stabilizing, GOOD)

**What to do when you see "positive feedback":**
✅ Ask yourself: "Does this amplify or dampen change?"
✅ If amplifies → it's positive feedback
✅ Remember: Positive ≠ Good; it just means amplifying

## TRAP #2: "Ozone Hole = Global Warming" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "The ozone hole is primarily responsible for global warming."

Student reasoning: "Ozone is in the atmosphere... CFCs are mentioned in both... so they must be related!"

**WRONG!** They are **mechanistically different**:

| Issue | Mechanism | Result | Gas |
|-------|-----------|--------|-----|
| **Ozone Depletion** | CFCs destroy O₃ in stratosphere | More UV-B reaches Earth → cancer/mutations | CFCs |
| **Global Warming** | GHGs trap infrared (heat) | Temperature rises | CO₂, CH₄ |

**Why the confusion?**
- CFCs ARE greenhouse gases (so they contribute to warming)
- But the ozone HOLE itself (loss of O₃) doesn't directly cause warming
- Different radiation types: UV vs. Infrared

**What to do:**
✅ If asked about the "ozone hole" → think UV-B, skin cancer, UV shield
✅ If asked about "global warming" → think infrared, heat trap, GHGs
✅ If they mention CFCs causing global warming → technically true (CFCs are GHGs) BUT the "hole" is about UV

**Example on exam:**
- Q: "The ozone hole causes global warming" → **FALSE**
- Q: "CFCs cause both ozone depletion AND contribute to global warming" → **TRUE** (but different mechanisms)

## TRAP #3: "Seasons are caused by distance from the Sun" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "Why does the Northern Hemisphere have winter in January?"
- Student: "Because we're farthest from the sun?"
- **WRONG!** Actually, Earth is **closest** to the sun (Perihelion) in January.

**Correct Answer:**
Seasons are caused by **axial tilt (23.5°)**:
- Jan (N. Hemisphere): North Pole tilted **AWAY** from sun → shallower angle → less intense → winter
- July (N. Hemisphere): North Pole tilted **TOWARD** sun → steeper angle → more intense → summer

**The Counter-Intuitive Fact:**

| Season (N.H.) | Month | Distance from Sun | Temperature | Why? |
|---------------|-------|------------------|-------------|------|
| Winter | January | CLOSEST (Perihelion) | COLDEST | Tilt → shallow angle |
| Summer | July | FARTHEST (Aphelion) | HOTTEST | Tilt → steep angle |

**What to do:**
✅ See "seasons" → immediately think "axial tilt"
✅ See "distance from sun" → recognize as a TRAP
✅ Remember: Australia has summer in our winter (proof: not about distance!)

## TRAP #4: "It's just a theory" (Science Illiteracy) ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "Which of these is 'just a theory' without strong evidence?"
Options include: Evolution, Gravity, Atomic Theory

**Student thinks:** "A theory is a guess, so evolution must be 'just' a theory"
**WRONG!** In science, a **theory is the HIGHEST level of confirmation**.

**Scientific Hierarchy:**
```
Observation → Hypothesis (Testable prediction)
   ↓
   Testing → Peer Review → More Testing → More Review
   ↓
THEORY (Broad explanation supported by massive evidence)
Examples: Evolution, General Relativity, Germ Theory, Quantum Mechanics
```

**Why "It's just a theory" is scientifically illiterate:**
- A theory means "supported by vast evidence," not "a guess"
- A hypothesis is the "guess" or tentative explanation
- The Scientific Method is iterative, not linear

**What to do:**
✅ See "theory" in question → recognize it as well-supported, not a guess
✅ If asked about hypothesis → that's the testable prediction, not proven yet
✅ Remember: "Scientific Theory ≠ Everyday Theory (guess)"

## TRAP #5: "Death and Birth Rates Fall Together in Demographic Transition" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "What causes rapid population growth in Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition?"
- Student thinks: "Both rates fall, so... they fall together?"
- **WRONG!** Death rates fall FIRST; birth rates lag 30-50 years.

**The Correct Model:**

| Stage | Death Rate | Birth Rate | Growth | Timeline |
|-------|-----------|-----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | High | High | Stable | Traditional society |
| 2 | **Falls** (medicine/sanitation) | High (culture unchanged) | **RAPID** | 30-50 years |
| 3 | Low | **Falls** (education/urbanization) | Slows | 30-50 years |
| 4 | Low | Low | Stable | Post-industrial |

**Why the lag?**
- Death rate drops instantly (medicine works immediately)
- Birth rate takes generations to drop (culture/education change slowly)
- Creates a 30-50 year gap of RAPID GROWTH

**What to do:**
✅ See "rapid population growth" → think "Stage 2: death ↓, birth stays ↑"
✅ See "demographic transition" → remember the LAG between death and birth
✅ Think about REAL examples: Sub-Saharan Africa (Stage 2, rapid growth)

## TRAP #6: "Science Proves Things" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "What is the primary goal of the scientific method?"
- Option A: "Prove theories beyond doubt"
- **WRONG!** Science doesn't provide absolute proof.

**The Truth:**
- Science provides **increasingly accurate approximations** based on evidence
- Science can **disprove** hypotheses (falsification)
- Science **cannot** provide absolute proof (100% certainty)

**Why?**
- We can never test every possible case
- New evidence can always change understanding
- Science is about reducing uncertainty, not eliminating it

**Example:**
- "Gravity is proven" ← Inaccurate. We understand gravity very well, but Newton's model was refined by Einstein.
- "Gravity is strongly supported by evidence and explains observations well" ← Accurate.

**What to do:**
✅ See "proof" or "proven" → recognize as non-scientific language
✅ Substitute: "strong evidence," "supported by data," "best explanation"
✅ Remember: Uncertainty is a feature of science, not a bug

## TRAP #7: "Heat Rises" ❌

**The Trap:**
Question: "Why does warm air in the troposphere rise?"
- Student: "Because heat rises"
- **INCOMPLETE!** The correct reason is **buoyancy (density difference)**, not "heat."

**The Correct Mechanism:**
- Warm air expands → becomes **less dense** than surrounding cooler air
- Less dense air is buoyant → floats upward
- It's the **density difference**, not "heat," that causes rising

**Why This Matters:**
- In the stratosphere, warm air stays near the bottom (different process: ozone absorbs UV)
- "Heat rises" is a hand-wavy explanation; "buoyancy due to density difference" is the real physics

**What to do:**
✅ See "rising air" or "convection" → think "density difference / buoyancy"
✅ Avoid "heat rises" as an explanation
✅ Remember: Density, not heat, drives convection

## IF YOU'RE UNSURE ON AN EXAM QUESTION...

1. **Read carefully**: What exactly is being asked?
2. **Check for trap language**: "positive," "ozone hole," "just a theory," "proof," "heat rises"
3. **Use the table above**: Which trap does this resemble?
4. **Think mechanistically**: Not "what sounds right" but "what's the actual mechanism?"
5. **When in doubt**: Choose the option that mentions:
   - **Feedback loops?** Pick the amplifying (positive) one if asking for destabilization
   - **Climate?** Pick the infrared/GHG option, not UV/ozone
   - **Seasons?** Pick the tilt option, not distance
   - **Science?** Pick "strong evidence," not "proof"
   - **Growth?** Pick the lag mechanism, not simultaneous changes
   - **Atmosphere?** Pick the buoyancy/density option
