## Week 1: SETI & The Fermi Paradox

### 🔴 MUST KNOW

- [ ] **What is the Fermi Paradox?**
  > The contradiction between the high probability of alien life (old galaxy, many stars) and the complete lack of evidence for it.

- [ ] **Name and explain the 4 main Fermi Paradox solutions:**
  > 1. **Rare Earth**: We are unique/alone; evolution of intelligent life is astronomically improbable
  > 2. **Zoo Hypothesis**: Aliens exist but deliberately hide/quarantine us (non-interference)
  > 3. **Dark Forest**: Aliens hide because the universe is dangerous — destroy others before being detected
  > 4. **Great Filter**: Some step in evolution is nearly impossible — either behind us (we're special) or ahead (we're doomed)

- [ ] **What distinguishes Zoo from Dark Forest?**
  > Zoo = Benevolent/indifferent observation; Dark Forest = Hostile survival strategy

### 🟡 SHOULD KNOW

- [ ] **What are the basic probability arguments for alien life?**
  > ~400 billion stars in Milky Way, galaxy is ~13 billion years old, Sun is only ~4.6 billion years old, galaxy could be colonized in ~10 million years at sub-light speeds

- [ ] **Why is the Great Filter's location important?**
  > If past: We're special, already made it through. If future: Civilizations tend to self-destruct — ominous for humanity

- [ ] **What does it mean if we find simple life elsewhere?**
  > Could be *bad news* — suggests the filter is ahead of us, not behind

### 🟢 NICE TO KNOW

- [ ] **What are technosignatures vs biosignatures?**
  > Technosignatures: Evidence of technology (radio signals, waste heat). Biosignatures: Evidence of life (oxygen, methane in atmosphere)

## Week 1-2: Chemistry of Life

### 🔴 MUST KNOW

- [ ] **Why is carbon the backbone of life?**
  > - 4 valence electrons → 4 stable bonds
  > - Can form double/triple bonds → chemical variety
  > - Forms long chains (polymers) → complex molecules
  > - CO₂ is a gas → easy to exhale/recycle

- [ ] **Why not silicon?**
  > - Weaker bonds, limited multiple bonds
  > - SiO₂ is solid (quartz) → "solid waste problem"

- [ ] **Why is water the solvent of life?**
  > - Polar molecule → dissolves many substances
  > - Wide liquid range (273-373K)
  > - Ice floats → insulates oceans, life survives below
  > - High heat capacity → temperature stability

- [ ] **What's special about "ice floats"?**
  > Water ice is less dense than liquid water → lakes freeze from top, liquid water remains below → life survives winter

### 🟡 SHOULD KNOW

- [ ] **What's the NASA working definition of life?**
  > "A self-sustained chemical system capable of undergoing Darwinian evolution"

- [ ] **Why is this definition problematic?**
  > It's a characteristic list, not a fundamental theory (like atomic theory for matter). Fire grows/consumes energy but isn't alive; mules can't reproduce but are alive.

- [ ] **What are extremophiles?**
  > Organisms that survive in extreme conditions (thermophiles in hot springs, psychrophiles in ice). They define the "biospace" — limits of where life can exist.

- [ ] **Why might methane (CH₄) be an alternative solvent?**
  > Non-polar, liquid at cold temperatures (Titan). BUT: solid methane sinks → lakes could freeze solid from bottom up.

### 🟢 NICE TO KNOW

- [ ] **What is a "shadow biosphere"?**
  > Hypothetical life on Earth using different biochemistry that we haven't detected yet

## Week 2-3: Stellar Astrophysics

### 🔴 MUST KNOW

- [ ] **List OBAFGKM from hottest to coolest:**
  > O → B → A → F → G → K → M
  > "Overwhelmingly Brilliant Astronomers Find Galaxies Kinda Mesmerizing"

- [ ] **What type is our Sun?**
  > G-type (yellow, ~5,500K, ~10 billion year lifespan)

- [ ] **What color are the hottest stars? Coolest?**
  > Hottest = Blue (O, B, >10,000K)
  > Coolest = Red (M, <3,500K)

- [ ] **Why do massive stars live shorter lives?**
  > They burn fuel much faster despite having more of it. High luminosity = rapid consumption.

- [ ] **What are typical lifespans?**
  > - O, B: ~10⁷ years (millions)
  > - G (Sun): ~10¹⁰ years (10 billion)
  > - M: >10¹¹ years (hundreds of billions)

### 🟡 SHOULD KNOW

- [ ] **Describe the H-R diagram:**
  > Plot of Luminosity (y-axis) vs Temperature (x-axis, reversed)
  > - Main Sequence: Diagonal band (O→M), actively fusing hydrogen
  > - Red Giants: Upper right (cool but huge, dying)
  > - White Dwarfs: Lower left (hot but tiny, dead cores)

- [ ] **What is the Main Sequence?**
  > Where stars spend most of their lives fusing hydrogen to helium. Sun is on the Main Sequence.

- [ ] **Problems with M-dwarf habitability:**
  > - Stellar flares (brightness spikes, radiation)
  > - Close habitable zone → intense exposure
  > - Possible tidal locking
  > - Variable brightness

### 🟢 NICE TO KNOW

- [ ] **Why are M-dwarfs still interesting targets?**
  > Most abundant stars, extremely long lifespans, nearest star (Proxima Centauri) is an M-dwarf with a planet in HZ

## Week 3: Planetary Habitability

### 🔴 MUST KNOW

- [ ] **What is the Habitable Zone?**
  > Region around a star where liquid water can exist on a planet's surface

- [ ] **Write the equilibrium temperature formula:**
  > $$T_{eq} = \left( \frac{(1-A)L}{16\pi\sigma a^2} \right)^{1/4}$$

- [ ] **What does each variable mean?**
  > - $T_{eq}$: Equilibrium temperature (K)
  > - $A$: Albedo (reflectivity, 0-1)
  > - $L$: Stellar luminosity (W)
  > - $\sigma$: Stefan-Boltzmann constant
  > - $a$: Orbital distance (m)

- [ ] **What's the #1 calculation mistake?**
  > Forgetting the **fourth root**! Also using (1+A) instead of (1-A).

- [ ] **What does $T_{eq}$ NOT account for?**
  > The greenhouse effect. Actual surface temperature is usually HIGHER than $T_{eq}$.

### 🟡 SHOULD KNOW

- [ ] **Conservative vs Optimistic HZ:**
  > - Conservative: Strict limits, high confidence
  > - Optimistic: Wider, includes atmospheric effects

- [ ] **What is the Faint Young Sun Paradox?**
  > The Sun was ~70% as bright 4 billion years ago, but Earth had liquid water. Resolved by: stronger early greenhouse effect (CO₂, methane).

- [ ] **How does the HZ change over time?**
  > Star brightens → HZ moves outward. In ~1 billion years, Earth will be too hot.

- [ ] **What is albedo? How does it affect temperature?**
  > Fraction of light reflected (0-1). High albedo → more reflection → LOWER temperature.

### 🟢 NICE TO KNOW

- [ ] **What happens during Red Giant phase?**
  > Star expands dramatically, luminosity increases 10x+, inner planets may be engulfed/sterilized

## Week 4: Exoplanet Detection

### 🔴 MUST KNOW

- [ ] **How does the transit method work?**
  > Planet passes in front of star → blocks some light → we see brightness dip

- [ ] **What can you measure from a transit?**
  > 1. Transit depth → Planet radius
  > 2. Time between transits → Orbital period
  > 3. Using Kepler's Law → Orbital distance

- [ ] **Write the transit depth equation:**
  > $$\text{Depth} = \left( \frac{R_{planet}}{R_{star}} \right)^2$$

- [ ] **How do you get planet radius?**
  > $$R_{planet} = R_{star} \times \sqrt{\text{Depth}}$$
  > (Don't forget the square root!)

- [ ] **What can transit NOT measure directly?**
  > Planet **mass**. Transit only measures size (radius).

- [ ] **Write Kepler's 3rd Law (simple form):**
  > $P^2 = a^3$ (P in years, a in AU)

### 🟡 SHOULD KNOW

- [ ] **What are the transit method biases?**
  > Favors: Large planets (deep dips), short periods (frequent transits), close orbits
  > Misses: Small planets, long periods, tilted orbits

- [ ] **What causes false positives?**
  > - Starspots (but don't repeat perfectly periodically)
  > - Eclipsing binaries
  > - Noise in data

- [ ] **What is limb darkening?**
  > Stars are brighter at center, dimmer at edges. Makes transit dip rounded, not flat.

- [ ] **How many transits needed to confirm a planet?**
  > At least 3 transits (2 complete intervals)

### 🟢 NICE TO KNOW

- [ ] **What are Hot Jupiters?**
  > Gas giants orbiting very close to their stars. Easy to detect but probably not habitable.

- [ ] **What are Super-Earths / Mini-Neptunes?**
  > Planets 1-4 Earth radii. Most common type found, but don't exist in our solar system!

## 🧮 Calculation Practice Checkpoints

### Equilibrium Temperature

- [ ] **Practice Problem**: A planet orbits at 1 AU from a Sun-like star ($L = L_\odot$) with albedo A = 0.3. Calculate $T_{eq}$.
  > Step 1: Plug into formula
  > Step 2: Don't forget (1-A) = 0.7
  > Step 3: Take fourth root!
  > Answer: ~255 K (for reference, Earth's observed is ~288 K due to greenhouse)

### Transit Calculations

- [ ] **Practice Problem**: A transit shows a 1% (0.01) depth around a Sun-like star. What's the planet's radius?
  > $R_{planet} = R_{star} \times \sqrt{0.01} = R_\odot \times 0.1 = 0.1 R_\odot$
  > This is roughly Jupiter-sized.

### Kepler's Law

- [ ] **Practice Problem**: A planet has orbital period P = 8 days. Calculate its distance from a Sun-like star.
  > Convert: 8 days = 8/365 years ≈ 0.022 years
  > $a^3 = P^2 = (0.022)^2 ≈ 0.00048$
  > $a = 0.078$ AU (very close! likely a Hot Jupiter)
